# Property Taxes & Transfer Fees Guide

## Overview
Buying, holding, and selling property in Pakistan involves several categories of tax and fee, in addition to the purchase price itself. This guide summarizes the general categories; buyers should confirm current rates with their developer, a tax advisor, or the relevant authority, as rates and filer/non-filer distinctions are periodically revised through the Finance Act.

## At Time of Purchase / Transfer
- **Stamp Duty:** Provincial duty payable on transfer of property, calculated as a percentage of the declared/DC value of the property, varying by province and city.
- **Registration Fee:** A separate fee for registering the transfer with the relevant land record/registration authority.
- **Withholding Tax on Purchase (Section 236K):** Federal withholding tax collected on the purchase of property, at different rates for active taxpayers ("filers") versus non-filers.
- **Capital Value Tax / Transfer Charges:** Additional provincial charges may apply depending on the city and property type.

## At Time of Sale (for Sellers)
- **Withholding Tax on Sale (Section 236C):** Federal withholding tax collected on the seller at time of sale, again with different rates for filers and non-filers.
- **Capital Gains Tax (CGT):** Payable on profit from the sale of property, with the rate depending on the holding period — properties held longer generally attract lower or no CGT compared to short-holding-period sales.

## Ongoing Holding Costs
- **Property Tax:** Annual provincial tax based on the assessed rental value of the property (for houses/plots with construction); vacant plots typically attract minimal or no annual property tax until developed.
- **Society/Community Maintenance Charges:** Recurring charges for shared facilities, security, and common-area upkeep, applicable in gated communities and apartment buildings (see each project's amenities section for what these charges typically fund).

## Filer vs. Non-Filer Status
Registering as an active taxpayer ("filer") with the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) generally results in meaningfully lower withholding tax rates on both purchase and sale of property compared to non-filer status. Buyers and sellers are encouraged to confirm their filer status and current applicable rates before a transaction, as these figures change with each Finance Act.

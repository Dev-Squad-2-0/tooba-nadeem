# Mortgage & Financing Guide

## Financing Options for Property Purchase in Pakistan

### 1. Developer Installment Plans
Many under-construction projects offer direct installment plans (see each project's payment plan section). These typically require a booking amount, a down payment, and a series of quarterly or monthly installments over 2-5 years, with a final payment on possession. No bank involvement or mark-up is charged by the developer in a standard installment plan, though late-payment surcharges may apply per the developer's own policy.

### 2. Conventional Bank Mortgage
Ready-to-move projects (fully constructed with clear title) are generally eligible for conventional home financing from commercial banks, subject to the bank's own valuation of the property, the buyer's income documentation, and the bank's lending criteria. Mortgage tenures commonly range from 5 to 20 years.

### 3. Islamic (Shariah-Compliant) Home Financing
Islamic banks offer Diminishing Musharakah-based home financing as an alternative to conventional interest-based mortgages, where the bank and buyer jointly own the property and the buyer's equity share increases over time through rental and purchase payments.

### 4. Government-Backed Housing Finance Schemes
From time to time, State Bank of Pakistan-supported low-mark-up housing finance schemes are available through partner banks for first-time buyers of smaller housing units. Availability and eligibility criteria change periodically; buyers should confirm current scheme terms directly with participating banks.

## What Buyers Should Prepare
- CNIC (and NICOP/passport for overseas Pakistanis)
- Proof of income/salary certificate or business bank statements (last 6-12 months)
- Property documents (allotment letter, payment plan, or title documents depending on project status)
- Down payment funds equal to the bank's required equity contribution (commonly 15-30% for conventional mortgages, varies by bank and property type)

## Important Note on Under-Construction Properties
Not all banks finance under-construction units, and those that do may release funds in tranches tied to construction milestones rather than as a lump sum. Buyers relying on bank financing for an under-construction project (e.g. Skyline Residency, Ocean Breeze Towers, Horizon Business Bay, Capital Greens Enclave, Al-Noor Valley) should confirm the bank's construction-linked disbursement policy before booking.
